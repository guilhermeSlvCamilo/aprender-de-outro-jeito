Aprender de Outro Jeito (ADJ)

Making school learning more accessible, one concept at a time.

Aprender de Outro Jeito is an open-source educational initiative designed to make school subjects more accessible to students with different learning needs, particularly students with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) and Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD).

The Problem

Traditional educational materials are not always accessible to every student. Long texts, complex language, excessive information and poorly structured explanations can create barriers to understanding and engagement.

Students deserve learning resources that adapt to different needs, learning paces and ways of processing information.

Our Vision

We aim to develop a platform that transforms traditional school content into clearer, simpler and more structured learning experiences.

The platform aims to:

- Simplify complex school subjects using accessible language.
- Break lessons into smaller, manageable learning steps.
- Provide practical examples connected to everyday life.
- Generate summaries highlighting key concepts.
- Create progressive exercises with explanatory feedback.
- Allow students to learn at their own pace.
- Explore customizable learning formats and experiences.

How It Works

The proposed system will allow educational content to be transformed into a structured learning experience:

1. Original Content: The student provides a school subject, topic or educational text.
2. Simplification: The content is rewritten using clearer language while preserving its meaning.
3. Step-by-Step Learning: The topic is divided into smaller sections.
4. Practical Examples: Concepts are connected to real-world situations.
5. Review: The student receives a concise summary.
6. Practice: Progressive exercises help reinforce learning.

Who Is It For?

- Students with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD).
- Students with Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD).
- Students who benefit from simplified and structured educational materials.
- Families and caregivers.
- Teachers and educators.

Accessibility Principles

We believe educational accessibility should be flexible and individualized.

The project aims to explore:

- Clear and concise language.
- Reduced cognitive overload.
- Predictable content structure.
- Flexible pacing.
- Visual organization.
- Optional learning formats.
- Respect for individual preferences.

These tools are intended to complement, not replace, teachers, educational professionals or individualized support.

Open Source

Our goal is to develop this project transparently and make its tools, code and documentation available for collaboration, adaptation and improvement.
